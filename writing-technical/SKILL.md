# The Bottom-Line First Technical Writing Rule

## Core Objective

Structure all technical and benchmark reports so critical findings and metrics appear first. Keep mechanical steps, configuration details, and tracking items near the end. Write with direct, objective, and simple syntax.

## Structural Requirements (Inverted Pyramid)

1. Lead with the bottom line.
   Open with core results and key metrics. Readers should know the outcome within the first few sentences.

2. Organize the body by implementation sequence.
   Place pipeline descriptions, performance breakdowns, and system metrics in the middle. Group related items together so the reader does not move back and forth.

3. Push bookkeeping to the tail.
   Put JIRA references, repo settings, historical links, and admin details at the end.

4. Consolidate caveats at the end.
   Group limits, infra constraints, and noise sources together, and call out optional effects or small sample caveats clearly.

## Style and Syntax Requirements

1. Eliminate all dashes.
   Do not use em dashes, en dashes, or double hyphens to connect clauses. Use commas, parentheses, or separate sentences.

2. Use direct verbs.
   Prefer direct copulas such as "is" and "are" over wordy constructions.

3. Use simple vocabulary.
   Avoid inflated terms like pivotal, showcase, underscore, testament, landscape, crucial, and delve. Use clear, direct verbs.

4. Keep a humble, balanced tone.
   Frame gains as conditional, identify limits clearly, and avoid unearned superlatives.
